# Raw Material Sourcing

Lithium-ion batteries need lithium, cobalt, nickel, and graphite. Roughly 70 percent of global
cobalt is mined in the Democratic Republic of Congo, much of it by a small number of large
industrial operations alongside informal artisanal mining.

## Concentration Risk

Because cobalt and lithium extraction are geographically concentrated, a disruption in one
country can affect global supply far more than a disruption in a typically diversified commodity
market. Automakers have responded by signing long-term offtake agreements directly with mining
companies instead of buying only on the spot market.

## Lower-Cobalt Chemistries

Battery chemistries such as LFP (lithium iron phosphate) avoid cobalt and nickel entirely, at
the cost of somewhat lower energy density. LFP has grown from a small share of the electric
vehicle battery market to a substantial one specifically because it reduces sourcing risk.
